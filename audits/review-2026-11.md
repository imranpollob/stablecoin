# Accounting and verification follow-up

Review date: **November 1, 2026 (America/New_York)**.

This internal review covers the three protocol contracts and their verification
harness, based on commit `132af51` and the subsequent remediation. It documents
a token-accounting issue, its fix, and improvements to invariant coverage.

## L-06 — Inherited allowance-based burn bypasses debt accounting

**Severity:** Low, self-assessed. **Status:** addressed in the current implementation.

### Preconditions and impact

`DecentralizedStableCoin` inherits OpenZeppelin `ERC20Burnable`. It overrides
`burn` with `onlyOwner`, but previously inherited unrestricted `burnFrom`.
A DSC holder could approve itself, or an approved spender, and burn tokens
without calling the engine. Total supply fell while borrower debt stayed unchanged:

```text
Before: activeDebt = 1000 DSC, badDebt = 0, supply = 1000 DSC
After burning 1 DSC directly: activeDebt = 1000 DSC, supply = 999 DSC
```

This breaks the intended debt/supply identity. It does not forgive borrower debt,
release collateral, or demonstrate theft: burning requires the holder's allowance
and consumes their tokens. The limited direct impact informs the Low rating.

### Remediation

Override `burnFrom` with `onlyOwner`, consistent with the existing mint/burn
policy. In the deployed model the owner is the engine, which does not expose an
arbitrary allowance-burn forwarding method. Repayment continues through
`engine.burnDsc`, which updates debt and burns matching DSC atomically.

### Executable evidence

```bash
forge test --match-contract L06DirectBurnTest -vv
```

The [regression](../test/poc/L06_DirectBurn.t.sol) checks holder self-approval,
approved third-party burning, and valid engine repayment. Temporarily removing
the new `burnFrom` override produced **two failing access-control regressions**;
restoring it makes all three tests pass. This checks that those tests distinguish
the missing control, rather than merely exercising a successful deployment.

## Verification harness corrections

The previous fail-on-revert handler never minted DSC. Its collateral-versus-supply
assertion therefore offered little borrowing coverage. The adversarial handler
also called owner-only token mint/burn paths and attempted deposits without the
borrower approval/prank setup needed for successful execution.

Current campaigns seed four funded borrowers with real engine-issued debt and
use explicit action selectors. The fail-on-revert campaign uses valid bounded
requests at fixed prices and checks debt conservation, custody, and health. The
adversarial setup also seeds a successful collateral-exhaustion settlement,
then permits price shocks, liquidation, repayment, and bad-debt coverage.
Its assertions concern ledger consistency; market losses may still occur.

The [property specification](../docs/properties.md) records exact assumptions.
The static-analysis documentation also replaces a previously unsupported
“all informational” result with a reproduced scan and visible severity counts.

| Executed check | Result |

| Local deployment script | Completes without broadcasting transactions |

The two stateful campaigns assert five properties in total; Foundry v1.8.4
reports each campaign as one aggregate test in its totals. The pinned older
`forge-std` also produces optional `targetInterfaces`/`excludeSelectors` probe
warnings; explicit action selectors and campaign metrics are verified.

## Remaining work

Broader rounding/decimal checks, multi-collateral liquidation liveness, fork
integration, composed governance/oracle campaigns, and independent verification
remain open. See the [roadmap](../README.md#development-roadmap). Restricting a token burn path does not
resolve insolvency, liquidation economics, or oracle trust.
